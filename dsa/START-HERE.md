## The one decision tree

```
Is there anything OVERDUE in ledger.md?
        │
    YES ├──→ Do ONLY the overdue reviews today. No new material. Stop.
        │
    NO  └──→ Go to your current week file → do the next unticked Day.
```

That's it. There is no third option. You never have to decide what to study.

---

## The 5 rules that override everything

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Blank page before notes. Always.** | Reading feels like learning. It isn't. Writing from memory is. |
| 2 | **20-minute cap per problem.** | Stuck at 20 min → open Hint 1. Still stuck at 30 → read the solution, log it in `mistakes.md`, move on. It comes back in 3 days anyway. |
| 3 | **120-minute cap per day. Hard stop.** | Even mid-problem. Finish it tomorrow. Burnout has ended more preps than difficulty. |
| 4 | **Max 3 new concepts a day.** | Not negotiable, not even on a good day. Extra time goes to *more problems*, never more concepts. |
| 5 | **Every mistake goes in `mistakes.md`.** | Untracked mistakes repeat. Tracked ones don't. |

## If you fall behind

Do this, in this order:

1. **Never cut review.** Overdue reviews are the highest-value 30 minutes you own.
2. Cut *problems* instead — drop the 🔵 stretch problems for that week entirely.
3. If still behind, add a day to the week. The schedule serves you; you don't serve it.

> A smaller syllabus you can actually recall beats the full syllabus you've forgotten. This trade-off is not close.

## What "done" means

A problem is **not** done when you've read the solution and it made sense.

A problem is done when you can **re-derive it cold**, from a blank editor, and say the approach out loud in 60 seconds.

Everything in this course exists to get you to that second definition.
