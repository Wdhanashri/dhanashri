## ① A-15 · Grammar — the eight rules that cover most errors

**Anchor:** *"Find the verb, find its subject, check they agree."*

Error-spotting questions test the **same eight things** over and over:

| # | Rule | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Subject–verb agreement** | "The list of items **is** long" — the subject is *list*, not *items* |
| 2 | **Each/every/either/neither/one of** → singular | "Each of the boys **has**", not *have* |
| 3 | **Tense consistency** | Don't switch tense mid-sentence without reason |
| 4 | **Preposition pairs** | different **from** · married **to** · superior **to** · comprises (no *of*) |
| 5 | **Articles** | a/an by **sound**: "an hour", "a university" |
| 6 | **Parallelism** | "She likes reading, writing **and swimming**" — same form throughout |
| 7 | **Misplaced modifier** | "Running fast, the bus was missed" — who was running? |
| 8 | **Pronoun agreement** | "Everyone must bring **his or her** bag" (formal), not *their* |

**The method that beats reading it aloud:**
```
1. Find the main VERB.
2. Find its SUBJECT — ignore everything between them.
3. Check they agree in number.
4. Then scan for prepositions and parallel lists.
```

**The Gotcha:** phrases like *"along with"*, *"as well as"*, *"including"*, *"in addition to"* **do not change the subject's number**. "The captain, along with the players, **is** here." That single rule appears in almost every paper.

**Recall:** *"The captain, along with the players, ___ here." Which verb, and why?*

## ② A-16 · Vocabulary — the highest-yield 20%

**Anchor:** *"You can't learn 5000 words. You can learn 30 roots and use the sentence."*

**When you don't know the word — three moves, in order:**
```
1. CONTEXT   — positive or negative? The sentence usually tells you.
2. ROOTS     — break the word apart.
3. ELIMINATE — kill the two you're sure are wrong, then guess. Never blank.
```

**The prefixes worth twenty minutes:**
```
a-/an- without        anti- against       ben- good         mal- bad
circum- around        contra- against     bene- well        mis- wrong
de- down/away         dis- apart          eu- good          pseudo- false
ex- out               inter- between      omni- all         sub- under
pre- before           post- after         super- above      trans- across
```

**The roots:**
```
-vert/-vers  turn      -dict  say        -spect  look      -port  carry
-scrib/script write    -ced/cess  go     -duc  lead        -fer  bring
-graph  write          -log  word        -path  feeling    -chron  time
```

*Malevolent* = mal (bad) + vol (wish) → **wishing harm.** You didn't need to have seen it.

**The Gotcha:** **no negative marking in most OAs** means a blank and a wrong answer score identically. Eliminating two options makes a guess a 50% shot. **Guessing is strictly better than skipping. Always.**

**Recall:** *You don't know the word. Name your three moves, in order.*

## ③ A-17 · Comprehension & para jumbles

**Anchor (RC):** *"Read the questions first. Then hunt — don't read."*

```
1. Skim the QUESTIONS first (10 seconds).
2. Skim the passage for STRUCTURE, not detail — what's each paragraph about?
3. For each question, go BACK and find the exact line. The answer is in the text.
4. Never use outside knowledge. "According to the passage" means only the passage.
```

**Answer types and how they're wrong:**
```
too EXTREME    — "always", "never", "all"        usually wrong
too NARROW     — true, but only one small detail  wrong for a main-idea question
outside scope  — true in real life, not stated    wrong
```

**Para jumbles — find the opener, then chain:**
```
1. The OPENER introduces something with a full noun ("A new study...").
   It never starts with a pronoun or a connector.
2. Pronouns (it, this, they) and connectors (however, therefore, moreover)
   must point BACKWARDS — so they can't be first.
3. Find mandatory PAIRS: a sentence naming X, then one saying "this X…"
4. Match against the options. Often two options share an opener —
   the pair-chain settles it.
```

**The Gotcha:** you rarely need the full correct order. **Find one mandatory pair and check which options contain it** — that usually eliminates three of four.

**Recall:** *In para jumbles, what can never be the opening sentence?*

## ⏱ SPEED DRILL — 14 questions, **35 seconds each**

```
GRAMMAR — find the error (or "no error")
 1.  The list of participants have been published.
 2.  Each of the students were given a book.
 3.  He is different than his brother.
 4.  She is superior than him in mathematics.
 5.  The team, along with the coach, are travelling today.
 6.  He likes swimming, running and to cycle.

VOCABULARY — meaning
 7.  Benevolent
 8.  Malicious
 9.  Circumvent
10.  Ephemeral
11.  Antonym of "candid"

TECHNIQUE
12.  Which sentence can NEVER open a para jumble: (a) "A new study shows…"
     (b) "However, this was later disproved."
13.  An RC option says "scientists have ALWAYS believed…". Likely right or wrong?
14.  You don't know the word and there's no negative marking. Skip or guess?
```

<details><summary>Answers + the rule behind each</summary>

1. **have → has** — subject is *list*
2. **were → was** — *each of* is singular
3. **than → from** — different **from**
4. **than → to** — superior **to**
5. **are → is** — *along with* doesn't change the subject
6. **to cycle → cycling** — parallelism
7. **kind, generous** — bene (good) + vol (wish)
8. **intending harm** — mal (bad)
9. **to go around / evade** — circum (around) + vent (come)
10. **short-lived**
11. **evasive / guarded** — candid = frank
12. **(b)** — starts with a connector pointing backwards
13. **Wrong** — too extreme
14. **Guess.** Always. A blank and a wrong answer score the same.
</details>

**Score:** correct __/14 · over 45 s → `mistakes.md`.
