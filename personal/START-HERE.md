# The first ten minutes — and the last

**4 files. ~4 hours total, spread over a week. Then 15 minutes before every interview.**

---

## Why this exists

DSA decides whether you get the offer. **This decides whether you get to show them your DSA.**

Every interview opens the same way: *"So, tell me about yourself"* → then whatever you mentioned gets pulled on. If those ten minutes go badly you spend the rest of the round recovering. If they go well, the interviewer is already on your side when the coding starts.

**And this is the cheapest thing in your whole prep.** The questions are known. The answers are about *you*, so there's nothing to learn — only to rehearse until it comes out clean.

## The 4 rules

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Never say anything you can't defend for two more questions.** | Everything you mention is an invitation. The resume is a menu, and so is your intro. |
| 2 | **Record yourself. Listen back.** | You cannot hear your own stalls, filler words, or trailing sentences while talking. You can hear them on playback, instantly. |
| 3 | **Answers are spoken, never read.** | Reading these files feels productive and changes nothing. Say every answer out loud, from memory, timed. |
| 4 | **"I don't know, but I'd find out by…" is a real answer.** | Bluffing on a technical detail is the fastest way to lose an interviewer. Admitting a gap cleanly costs you almost nothing. |

---

## The one thing to get right

**Your intro is a menu you hand the interviewer.**

Whatever you name, they will ask about — so name only what you want to be asked, and deliberately plant the hooks you're strongest on. Mention preCICE and the maintainer review, and you've steered the next ten minutes onto ground where almost no other candidate can follow you.

That's the whole strategy. `intro.md` builds it.

## Before every interview — 15 minutes

1. `intro.md` — say the 60-second answer twice, out loud.
2. `questions.md` — the Big 5, out loud.
3. Skim your **two** deep files (`open-source.md`, `cartwise.md`) — the 30-second versions only.
4. Re-read the job description and pick **one** line to reference in "why this company".

**Now open `intro.md`.**
