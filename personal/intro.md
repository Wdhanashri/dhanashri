# "Tell me about yourself" — the first 60 seconds

**~60 min to build. Then two minutes before every interview, forever.**

---

## ⓪ Pretest — 2 min. **Write a guess before reading on.**

1. You have 60 seconds. What are the three things you'd say?
2. Which single fact about you is least likely to be on any other candidate's resume?

> Write both down. Wrong is fine — the answer lands harder after you've committed to a guess.

---

## ① The idea that makes this easy

**Anchor:** *"Your intro is a menu. They order from it."*

Whatever you name, they will ask about next. So:

```
❌ list everything you've ever done   →  they pull the thread YOU'RE weakest on
✅ name three things you can defend   →  the next ten minutes happen on your ground
```

**This is the whole strategy.** You are not summarising your resume — they have it. You are **choosing which three doors to open.**

Your three doors, and they're not close:

```
1. CartWise        a real full-stack system with hard integrations
2. preCICE         4 merged PRs, maintainer review  ← almost no fresher has this
3. What you want   one sentence, so it doesn't sound like a shrug
```

---

## ② Your answer — the shape

```
PRESENT   who you are right now, in one line       ~8 sec
BUILT     your strongest project, concretely       ~20 sec
DIFFERENT the thing nobody else has                ~20 sec
WANT      what you're looking for                  ~8 sec
```

**Total: 55–70 seconds.** Longer and they stop listening; shorter and it reads as unprepared.

---

## ③ The draft — make it yours

> "I'm Dhanashri, a final-year B.Tech student in Computer Science with AI at Parul University in Vadodara.
>
> Most of my hands-on work has been full-stack. My main project is **CartWise**, an AI-powered grocery app — I built the FastAPI backend with Supabase, added OCR bill scanning and LLM-based health recommendations, and integrated Razorpay for payments and Google Maps for nearby store discovery.
>
> Alongside that I've been contributing to open source. I have **four merged pull requests** into the **preCICE** documentation site, which the multi-physics simulation community uses. That one taught me the most — working inside a codebase I didn't write, and getting changes through maintainer review, felt closer to real engineering than anything else I'd done.
>
> I'm looking for an SDE role where I can keep doing that kind of end-to-end work."

**Read it aloud once with a timer.** Then rewrite it in your own words — the phrasing has to sound like you, not like a script, and the only way to get there is to say it until it's yours.

### Why it's built this way

| Line | Doing what |
|---|---|
| "final-year… CS with AI" | context in one breath. No school history, no childhood. |
| CartWise with **named** technologies | concrete beats adjectives. "FastAPI and Supabase" lands; "worked on a full-stack project" doesn't. |
| **"four merged pull requests"** | the number is the hook. *Merged* is the word that matters — it means someone reviewed and accepted it. |
| "taught me the most… maintainer review" | invites the question you most want: *"tell me about that."* |
| "end-to-end work" | answers "what do you want" without sounding like you'd take anything. |

**The Gotcha:** don't say *"I'm a hard worker"*, *"I'm passionate about technology"*, or *"I'm a quick learner."* Every candidate says these and they carry zero information. **Every sentence should be something only you could say.**

## ⑤ The two that come straight after

These almost always follow the intro. Have them ready in the same breath.

### "Why do you want to join us?"

**Anchor:** *"One thing about them, one thing about you, one sentence joining them."*

```
❌ "It's a great company with good growth opportunities."       ← says nothing
✅ "I read that your team works on <specific thing>. I've been
    doing <your related thing>, and I'd like to do that at
    a scale I can't reach on my own projects."
```

**Do this the night before**, per company. Ten minutes on their careers page or engineering blog. **One specific detail is enough** — and it's noticed precisely because most candidates skip it.

### "What are your strengths?"

**Give two, each with evidence.** A strength without evidence is a claim; with evidence it's a fact.

> *"Two things. First, I'm comfortable in a codebase I didn't write — the preCICE work was entirely in someone else's Jekyll site and I had to read a lot before changing anything.
>
> Second, I follow things through to actually shipping. CartWise wasn't a demo — payments work, auth works, it runs. I'd rather finish one thing properly than start three."*

**The Gotcha:** "I'm a perfectionist" and "I work too hard" are recognised as dodges. Give a real strength with a real example.

## ⑥ Drill it — 20 min

**Record every attempt on your phone. Listen back to each one.**

```
Round 1   read it aloud from the page. Time it.
Round 2   close the file. Say it from memory. Time it.
Round 3   say it while walking around. (Harder. Closer to a real room.)
Round 4   say it as if to a friend, not an interviewer — this kills the
          script-reading tone, which is what makes people sound nervous.
Round 5   listen to round 4. Write down every filler word you hear.
```

**Target:** 55–70 seconds, no "um", no trailing sentences, no apology in your voice.

**What to listen for on playback:**
```
"umm" / "like" / "basically"       →  usually means you're improvising a link
your voice rising at the end       →  makes statements sound like questions
speed                              →  nerves make you fast. Deliberately slow down.
trailing off                       →  finish the last word of every sentence
```
